Who’s Behind This Rabbit Hole?

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent, not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm a neurospicy person with a lot of questions.

I bring professional and lived experience, intense hyperfocus and internet access. The sources are linked so you can keep reading, question my take and decide what’s useful for you.

“Why do I keep coming back to the music room?”

Because it’s where I teach. It’s where a wrong note can feel enormous, a useful routine can make the whole afternoon easier and a student can discover they have something to contribute.

I’m interested in what makes those experiences possible. The way we give feedback. The resources a student can reach without asking. The difference between offering a choice and disguising a demand as one.

My experience as an educator, a neurodivergent person and a parent shapes the questions I ask. Your family or classroom may bring different questions. There’s room for that here.

“And then I went looking for research.”

Music education, learning from mistakes, demand avoidance, motivation, repetitive thoughts: one question tends to lead to about twelve more. Hence the rabbit hole.

I’ve linked the studies and resources that inform my thinking. Some explore classrooms; others involve adults, laboratories or therapy settings. I try to explain those differences where they matter to the idea we’re discussing.

Failure First is the name I use for my teaching approach to mistakes and recovery. The related research helps me think about it, and the classroom is where I have to work out what useful support actually looks like.

Browse the bibliography and further reading. Follow the original work, question the connections and decide what’s useful in your situation.

“Mistakes are information. That applies to me, too.”

If you spot an error or have a source you think belongs in the conversation, let me know. I want this collection to keep getting more useful.

Every brain belongs in music.

Sarah Batchelor · Neurospicy Music Teacher
