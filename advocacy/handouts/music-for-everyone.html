Music Is for Everyone

A resource for PDA and neurodiversity advocacy: appropriate inclusion, useful supports and music programs for every learner.

Download the handout

Music is learning.

Students can learn to listen, create, perform and understand music. Musical learning has value in its own right. Access should not depend on a promise that music will improve scores in another subject.

Inclusion needs usable support.

An adapted part, instrument loan, accessible materials, sensory options and clear routines can make participation more reachable. Adults can teach students how to use those supports and remain available as needed.

What could a parent ask about?

Inclusion in an existing class, using an available music opportunity within a support plan, or adding an inclusive class, ensemble or club. The education team can discuss staffing, space, costs and how to review it.

Sources and further reading

Why Does Your School Need Music? connects the research and its limits.

Habibi et al. (2018) report musically relevant auditory learning and associated brain changes. Jamey et al. (2024) examine inhibitory control. These studies look at particular aspects of learning; the student’s experience of the class matters alongside them. CAST UDL Guidelines offer design guidance for accessible learning.
