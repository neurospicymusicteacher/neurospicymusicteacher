Music, Singing and Repetitive Thoughts

Explore a possible reprieve without promising to switch worry off.

Initial draft · 25 November 2026

Download PDF

Use the accurate claim

Singing may offer some people a temporary break from worry. Research does not establish that worrying while singing is impossible. Adult singing findings and memory experiments do not prove a PDA-specific intervention.

Offer, do not prescribe

Options might include private humming, a familiar instrumental pattern, beat-making, chosen listening or quiet. Agree on volume, setting, supervision and how to stop. Public singing may increase anxiety for some students.

Ask about the effect

An optional check-in: more settled, the same or more stirred up? Do not require disclosure of private thoughts. Notice delayed exhaustion as well as immediate responses.

Mood and rumination differ

A randomized listening experiment improved mood without a significant between-condition difference in rumination. Feeling better does not prove that repetitive thoughts stopped.

Address the environment

Keep appropriate mental-health support available. Music does not remove bullying or other ongoing causes of distress. Change the conditions as well as offering ways to cope.

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent, not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm just a neurospicy person with intense hyperfocus, professional and lived experience, and access to the internet.

My articles explore research through an educational and lived-experience lens. They are intended to inform, encourage curiosity and offer practical ideas, not to provide clinical advice. I encourage you to explore the original research linked throughout.
