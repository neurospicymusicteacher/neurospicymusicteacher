Meet Sarah

Practical, honest, neuro-affirming professional learning.

Sarah Batchelor is a Canadian music educator, speaker, mentor, and creator of Neurospicy Music Teacher. With 25 years of experience teaching music, band, and K–12 classrooms, Sarah brings a practical, honest, and neuro-affirming voice to professional learning.

Her sessions are grounded in real classroom experience, lived experience as an educator with autism and ADHD, and a deep belief that music education should work for everyone in the room — including the teacher.

Sarah’s goal is to be the teacher, mentor, and advocate she needed when she was growing up: someone who validates neurodivergent experiences, makes space for different ways of thinking, and helps educators build classrooms where every brain belongs.

From Outcomes to the Rehearsal Room: Making Alberta’s Instrumental Music Curriculum Usable

A practical session that turns Alberta’s Instrumental Music 7–9 Program of Studies from a dense curriculum document into a useful roadmap for planning, teaching, rehearsing, and assessing.

Click for more ✨

Alberta’s Instrumental Music curriculum contains strong ideas, but translating broad outcomes into the daily reality of a band room can be overwhelming. What does an outcome actually look like in rehearsal? Where does it fit across the year? How can teachers build a program that is musically meaningful, developmentally appropriate, and realistic within limited rehearsal time?

Drawing from Sarah’s Curriculum Companion to the Instrumental Music 7–9 Alberta Program of Studies, this session breaks the curriculum into practical teaching priorities. Participants will explore how technical skills, musicianship, creativity, performance, reflection, and assessment can work together instead of becoming separate boxes to check.

The session also considers Grade 6 beginner band programs that feed into Grades 7–9, helping teachers build a coherent progression from students’ first sounds through more independent ensemble playing. Participants will leave with clear ways to connect curriculum outcomes to repertoire, rehearsal routines, assessment, and long-range planning.

Best suited for

Alberta band teachers, Grade 6 beginner band teachers, junior high music educators, new band teachers, school divisions, music education conferences, and teachers revising their program plans or assessment practices.

Key topics

Alberta’s Instrumental Music 7–9 Program of Studies, Grade 6–9 skill progression, long-range planning, rehearsal design, repertoire connections, assessment, student-friendly outcomes, scope and sequence, and practical curriculum implementation.

Participants receive a one-page curriculum overview they can use immediately, with additional companion resources available for those who want a complete planning and assessment system.

Failure First: A Growth Mindset Approach to Beginner Band

A practical beginner band session about helping students build confidence, resilience, and independence by normalizing mistakes, celebrating progress, and creating systems that support successful risk-taking from the very beginning.

Click for more ✨

Beginner band is messy, loud, exciting, and full of opportunities to make mistakes. Before students can create beautiful sounds, they need permission to squeak, miss notes, lose their place, try again, and discover that an imperfect attempt is evidence of learning—not proof that they “aren’t musical.”

In this session, Sarah shares practical ways to establish a growth-mindset culture before students ever put instruments in their hands. Participants will examine how language, routines, instrument selection, family communication, progress tracking, and classroom systems can reduce fear of failure and help young musicians become resilient problem-solvers.

The goal is not to lower expectations. It is to create the conditions students need to take healthy risks, respond to feedback, build independence, and keep going when learning feels difficult.

Best suited for

Beginner band teachers, elementary and junior high music teachers, new band teachers, music education conferences, professional learning days, and teachers rebuilding or rethinking their beginner band programs.

Key topics

Growth mindset, productive failure, beginner band setup, instrument selection, first sounds, resilience, student independence, parent communication, progress tracking, classroom culture, and support for students with additional needs.

Fix the Barriers, Fix the Behaviors

A practical session about reducing challenging behaviours in music and band classes by identifying the access barriers underneath them—including reading overload, executive functioning demands, sensory overwhelm, anxiety, and fear of failure.

Click for more ✨

Sometimes it’s not the kid—it’s the system. Many behaviours we interpret as defiance, laziness, inattention, or lack of care are actually signs that a student has encountered a barrier they do not yet have the tools to navigate.

Music classrooms place enormous demands on working memory, reading, listening, motor coordination, emotional regulation, sensory processing, and executive functioning—all at the same time. This session helps educators look beneath the behaviour, identify what is getting in the way, and make targeted changes that preserve high expectations while making success more accessible.

Participants will explore practical supports including modified notation, learning sheets, visual routines, predictable rehearsal structures, reduced cognitive load, meaningful choices, sensory supports, and systems that build student independence. The session also introduces assigning instruments through the lens of diagnosis, considering how individual learning, sensory, and physical needs may affect instrument fit. The focus is on realistic changes educators can use immediately in busy music classrooms.

The instrument-assignment component is an introduction to a topic Sarah is currently developing into its own full professional learning session.

Best suited for

Music educators, band teachers, elementary music teachers, inclusive education teams, school leaders, convention sessions, professional learning days, and teachers working with complex classrooms.

Key topics

Access barriers, behaviour as communication, modified notation, learning sheets, executive functioning, predictable routines, sensory needs, anxiety, instrument assignment through the lens of diagnosis, Universal Design for Learning, cognitive load, student independence, and classroom systems.

Available in two formats: In the one-hour lecture-style session, Sarah demonstrates how to adapt printed music so students with dyslexia can read it more easily, along with practical strategies for removing other common barriers. In the two-hour interactive workshop, participants should bring their own device and will have guided working time to try the process themselves by creating adapted music parts and learning sheets for their own students.

Embracing the Chaos - Surviving as a Neurodiverse Educator

A practical and personal session exploring neurodiversity in education, the hidden costs of masking and “coping,” and what it can look like for neurodivergent educators to move from surviving toward a more sustainable way of teaching.

Click for more ✨

Teaching asks educators to manage constant noise, interruptions, changing expectations, competing priorities, emotional labour, and a workload that rarely fits neatly inside the school day. For educators with autism, ADHD, or other neurodivergent ways of thinking, those demands can create both remarkable strengths and very real challenges.

Grounded in Sarah’s lived experience as an educator with autism and ADHD, this session explores what neurodiversity can look like in the adults working inside schools. It examines the hidden labour of masking, the difference between appearing to cope and actually being supported, and the systems educators often build quietly just to keep functioning.

Participants will reflect on organization, sensory needs, executive functioning, communication, creativity, burnout, and the value of supportive professional relationships. The session offers practical strategies for building sustainable routines while also inviting schools and leaders to consider how workplace culture can make it safer for neurodivergent educators to ask for what they need.

Best suited for

Teachers, school leaders, music educators, convention sessions, inclusion-focused professional learning, staff wellness conversations, and neurodivergent educators seeking practical validation and support.

Key topics

Neurodiversity, autism and ADHD in educators, masking, burnout, coping versus thriving, sensory needs, executive functioning, routines, organization, creativity, support networks, and school culture.
