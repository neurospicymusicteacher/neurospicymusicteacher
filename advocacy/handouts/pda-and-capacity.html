“But They Did It Yesterday.”

A parent handout about PDA, autonomy and why today may need different support.

Download the one-page handout

Start here: what is PDA?

PDA usually means Pathological Demand Avoidance. Some people prefer Persistent Drive for Autonomy. Families use it to describe intense difficulty with demands. Researchers are still debating how to define and assess the profile.

1. Describe what happens, not just the label.

Does “come and ask me” make getting help harder? Does the same task go differently when the room is quieter? Write down the demand, the setting and what your child says about it.

2. Ask for fewer hurdles.

A shared charger, spare music or instructions on the board can remove a whole conversation. Agree on honest choices: start here or there, use this format or that one. An adult can help your child learn the routine.

3. Make room for today.

Knowing how to do something and being able to manage it right now are different things. Ask what support is available on a harder day. Yesterday is useful information. It is not an invoice due today.

Words you can borrow

They know how to [skill]. What seems to get in the way today is [barrier]. Could we try [support]?
