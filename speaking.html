Meet Sarah

Practical, honest, neuro-affirming professional learning.

Sarah Batchelor is a Canadian music educator, speaker, mentor, and creator of Neurospicy Music Teacher. With 25 years of experience teaching music, band, and K–12 classrooms, Sarah brings a practical, honest, and neuro-affirming voice to professional learning.

Her sessions are grounded in real classroom experience, lived experience as an educator with autism and ADHD, and a deep belief that music education should work for everyone in the room — including the teacher.

Sarah’s goal is to be the teacher, mentor, and advocate she needed when she was growing up: someone who validates neurodivergent experiences, makes space for different ways of thinking, and helps educators build classrooms where every brain belongs.

Embracing the Chaos

Neurodivergent Educators, Support, and Sustainability

A practical and personal session exploring neurodiversity in education, the hidden costs of masking and “coping,” and what it can look like for neurodivergent educators to move from surviving to thriving.

View details

This session invites educators and school communities to think more deeply about how schools support neurodivergent staff. Grounded in lived experience, research, and real classroom practice, Sarah explores what neurodiversity is, how it can show up in educators, and why “but you’re managing” is not the same as meaningful support.

Participants will reflect on the strengths and challenges of neurodivergent thinking, the hidden labour of masking, and practical strategies that can make teaching more sustainable. This session is honest, affirming, and rooted in the belief that educators deserve support systems that honour how they actually work.

Best suited for

Teachers, school leaders, music educators, convention sessions, inclusion-focused professional learning, and staff wellness conversations.

Key topics

Neurodiversity, masking, burnout, coping vs. thriving, educator sustainability, sensory needs, routines, organization, creativity, support networks, and school culture.

Failure First

Growth Mindset Approaches to Beginner Band

A practical beginner band session about helping students build confidence, resilience, and independence by normalizing mistakes, celebrating progress, and creating systems that help young musicians succeed from the very beginning.

View details

Beginner band is messy, loud, exciting, and full of opportunities to make mistakes. In this session, Sarah shares practical strategies for helping students understand that “bad sounds” are not failure — they are part of learning.

Inspired by years of watching students become increasingly hesitant to try new things, Failure First explores how music teachers can intentionally create classrooms where students take chances, make mistakes, problem-solve, and grow. The session looks at what happens before students even get instruments in their hands, how to support instrument selection, how to communicate with families, and how to build systems that help students become more resilient and self-reliant.

Participants will leave with practical ideas for beginner band routines, student independence, parent communication, tracking progress, positive behaviour supports, and supporting students with additional needs in complex music classrooms.

Best suited for

Beginner band teachers, elementary and junior high music teachers, new band teachers, music education conferences, professional learning days, and teachers rebuilding or rethinking their beginner band programs.

Key topics

Growth mindset, beginner band setup, instrument selection, first sounds, resilience, student independence, parent communication, band binders, tracking progress, PBIS in the music room, and supporting students with additional needs.

From Squeaks to Symphonies

Setting Beginner Band Students Up for Success

A practical beginner band session about building the structures, routines, and early experiences that help students move from first sounds and squeaks toward confidence, independence, and long-term success.

View details

The first weeks of beginner band can feel loud, chaotic, and more than a little unhinged — shiny new instruments, big feelings, and sounds no one warned you about. But with intentional structures in place, those early squeaks are not just something to survive. They are the starting point for confident, capable young musicians.

In this session, Sarah shares practical systems for setting students up for success before the first note is ever played. Participants will explore ways to slow the start, reduce overwhelm, normalize the learning curve, and remove unnecessary barriers so students can make progress, take risks, and stay engaged in band.

This session includes ideas for instrument selection, student fit, percussion decisions, instrument petting zoos, first sounds, small-group instrument introductions, practice strategies, progress tracking, and routines that help beginner band students build both skill and resilience. The focus is not on pushing harder or moving faster — it is on building a foundation that helps music work for everyone in the room.

Best suited for

Beginner band teachers, elementary and junior high music teachers, new band teachers, music education conferences, professional learning days, and teachers wanting stronger systems for the first weeks and months of band.

Key topics

Beginner band setup, first sounds, instrument selection, instrument petting zoo, Squeak and Squawk events, percussion placement, student fit, practice routines, resilience, cognitive load, self-efficacy, progress tracking, and classroom systems.

Fix the Barriers, Fix the Behaviors

Accessible Music Classrooms, Student Needs, and Practical Supports

A practical session about reducing challenging behaviours in music and band classes by identifying the access barriers underneath them — including reading overload, executive functioning demands, sensory overwhelm, anxiety, and fear of failure.

View details

Sometimes it’s not the kid — it’s the system.

In this session, Sarah explores the idea that many “behaviours” we see in music classrooms are not defiance, laziness, or lack of care. They are often the result of students running into barriers they do not yet have the tools to navigate. In band and music class, those barriers can include notation that is too visually overwhelming, unclear rehearsal routines, executive functioning demands, sensory overload, reassurance-seeking, anxiety, physical access needs, or fear of being wrong in front of peers.

This session is not a behaviour management program, and it is not about lowering expectations. It is a guided tour through practical, classroom-tested adaptations that make music more accessible, more playable, and more successful for a wider range of learners. Participants will explore how modified notation, learning sheets, predictable routines, environmental supports, student need profiles, and accessible entry points can reduce overwhelm and increase meaningful participation.

This is a “there is no way we can cover all of this in one hour, but we are absolutely going to try” kind of session. It can stand alone as a high-impact overview, or it can be expanded into multiple deeper sessions on accessibility, notation supports, learning sheets, executive functioning, sensory needs, and classroom systems.

Best suited for

Music educators, band teachers, elementary music teachers, inclusive education teams, school leaders, convention sessions, professional learning days, and teachers working with complex classrooms.

Key topics

Access barriers, behaviour as communication, modified notation, learning sheets, executive functioning, predictable routines, sensory needs, reassurance-seeking, anxiety, student independence, adaptive instruments, Universal Design for Learning, cognitive load, and practical classroom systems.

This session is currently offered as a high-impact overview and can also be expanded into a longer workshop or multi-session professional learning series.
