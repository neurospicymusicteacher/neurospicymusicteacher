For parents and caregivers · PDA, neurodiversity & music

“Does My Child Belong in Music?”

Yes. Let’s talk about what makes participation possible, including PDA and neurodivergent learners.

Download the one-page handout

1. Start with what they want to learn.

Trumpet? Singing? Making beats? Playing with a group? Music education is a chance to develop something your child cares about. It does not have to earn its place by fixing another subject.

2. Ask for access that means something.

“They can sit at the back” is not the whole plan. What part can they learn? Who will teach them? Could an adapted part, instrument loan, predictable routine or sound adjustment help them take part?

3. If music is missing, ask how it could begin.

A class, ensemble or creative music group needs teaching time, an educator, space and resources. Ask how costs, transport and support will be handled so the same children are not left outside again.

Words you can borrow

My child wants to [musical goal]. What could we put in place so they can learn and take part?
