Music Is More Than Enrichment

“Does music have to raise a math score to matter?” Come explore the research—and the case for belonging.

By Sarah Batchelor · Draft created · Sources checked 25 November 2026

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent — not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm just a neurospicy person with intense hyperfocus, professional and lived experience, and access to the internet.

My articles explore research through an educational and lived-experience lens. They are intended to inform, encourage curiosity and offer practical ideas, not to provide clinical advice. I encourage you to explore the original research linked throughout.

Music does not need to improve a mathematics score to deserve a place in school.

Learning to make, understand and share music has educational value in its own right. Students deserve access to that learning, including students whose participation requires adjustments. The research on other possible benefits can strengthen a conversation, but it should not become the only reason music is allowed to matter.

A credible advocacy case is specific about what was studied. Music education, listening to a recording and clinical music therapy are different activities. Evidence from one cannot automatically be used to promise the effects of another.

What can we reasonably say?

A 2024 meta-analysis found benefits for inhibitory control across controlled longitudinal music-training studies. That is a specific outcome, not proof of improvement in every executive function, school attainment or everyday difficulty. The included studies do not establish a music intervention for PDA.

Habibi and colleagues' longitudinal research review describes changes in musically relevant auditory skills and associated brain development. Brain differences are not a shortcut to claims about higher intelligence or better grades.

A review of music interventions and stress found average reductions in psychological and physiological stress measures across varied settings. An average benefit is not a guarantee that a noisy rehearsal will feel calming to an individual student.

Include the inconvenient evidence

Sala and Gobet's meta-analysis challenges broad claims about transfer from music training to general cognitive and academic skills when study quality is taken into account. It belongs in this hub alongside more positive findings.

Different reviews can examine different outcomes and study sets. A benefit on an inhibition task and a lack of broad academic transfer are not the same question. Our job is to keep those questions separate, rather than selecting whichever headline makes music sound most impressive.

That makes the advocacy more durable. A school should not be able to dismiss access to musical learning because a promised increase in test scores did not appear.

Education and therapy have different purposes

The 2022 Cochrane review found probable benefits of music therapy for overall improvement and quality of life among autistic participants, with uncertain effects on social interaction and communication. Those findings concern music therapy delivered within the included studies, not ordinary band instruction.

For clinical goals, involve an appropriately qualified music therapist. For music education, provide skilled teaching and accessible participation. A student may benefit from both services, but calling all musical activity therapy blurs the expertise and purpose of each.

What families can ask for

Begin with the student's interests and barriers. Could they access curricular music with an adapted part? Would a small creative group, digital production or a supervised music-room break be useful? Is there an adult who can make the opportunity predictable and welcoming?

These are proposed options to explore, not a prescribed package. Ask about volume, staffing, cost, transport, equipment and assessment. An opportunity that exists on paper may still be difficult to reach.

Measure what matters locally

A small school pilot might consider musical growth, student-reported comfort, voluntary engagement and meaningful contribution. Attendance can be part of the picture, but more attendance alone does not establish improved well-being.

Keep the student's account central and make it possible to say an activity is not helping. Music is powerful enough to deserve careful teaching and honest evidence. We do not need to turn it into a cure, a compliance tool or a guarantee of success in every other subject to defend its place.

Sources and evidence limits

  • Jamey et al. (2024). Does music training improve inhibition control in children? A systematic review and meta-analysis. Cognition, 252, 105913. Systematic review and meta-analysis
  • Habibi et al. (2018). Music training and child development: A review of recent findings from a longitudinal study. Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences. DOI: 10.1111/nyas.13606. Review of a longitudinal program
  • de Witte et al. (2020; first online 2019). Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: A systematic review and two meta-analyses. Health Psychology Review, 14, 294–324. Systematic review and meta-analyses
  • Geretsegger et al. (2022). Music therapy for autistic people. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, CD004381. Cochrane systematic review
  • Sala & Gobet (2020). Cognitive and academic benefits of music training with children: A multilevel meta-analysis. Memory & Cognition, 48, 1429–1441. Meta-analysis
