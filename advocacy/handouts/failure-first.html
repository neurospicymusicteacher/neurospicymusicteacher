“What If a Mistake Was Just Information?”

Help the school teach what to do after something goes wrong, in music and in other classrooms.

Download the one-page handout

A wrong note is information.
It is not a character reference.

1. Find out what happens after the mistake.

When your child loses their place, forgets music or answers incorrectly, what comes next? A correction they can use? A public reminder? Ten minutes trying not to cry? Start with their experience.

2. Ask for a route back in.

“That should be F-sharp” gives information. “You never pay attention” adds a judgement. Ask for a clear next step, a quieter cue or a resource they can reach themselves. The spare-music binder should not come with a lecture.

3. Make the same route available elsewhere.

If grabbing spare music works in band, could spare worksheets work in math? The other teacher needs to provide the resource and teach the routine, too. “You can do it in band” is not the missing support.

Words you can borrow

When [mistake] happens, could the next step be [usable help], so they can get back to learning?
