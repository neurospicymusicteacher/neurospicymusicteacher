A Music Support Schools Can Actually Run

Choose a feasible route and give someone responsibility for making it accessible.

Initial draft · 25 November 2026

Download PDF

Protect curricular music

Review adapted parts, accessible notation, practice support and assessment options. Keep meaningful teaching in every role. Composition and technical work should be choices with instruction, not permanent assignments based on assumptions.

Explore additional routes

Consider a low-pressure club, supervised open room, student-led band, songwriting, rhythm and movement or digital production. Preserve quiet alternatives and check which activities the student wants.

Make access concrete

Agree on staffing, timetable, instruments, fees, transport, sound levels and a backup plan. Check physical and communication access. A room that exists is not automatically a room the student can use.

Name the purpose

Is this curriculum instruction, an agreed regulation break, leisure or clinical music therapy? Clarify the purpose so assessment and access match it. Consult a qualified therapist for clinical goals.

Keep support available

Do not turn an agreed accommodation into an earned reward. Safety and supervision still apply; when one activity cannot proceed, plan another way to meet the need where possible.

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent — not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm just a neurospicy person with intense hyperfocus, professional and lived experience, and access to the internet.

My articles explore research through an educational and lived-experience lens. They are intended to inform, encourage curiosity and offer practical ideas, not to provide clinical advice. I encourage you to explore the original research linked throughout.
