How Can We Advocate for PDA & Neurodivergent Learners?

A parent guide to understanding PDA, school supports, music inclusion and new music opportunities.

Download the six-page guideStart with Step 1

What is this guide for?

Use it to help the education team understand your child, discuss supports across educational settings, explore music inclusion or advocate for new music opportunities. The team provides resources, teaching and access so students can participate now and develop ways to support themselves over time.

What is PDA?

PDA commonly means Pathological Demand Avoidance. Some people prefer Persistent Drive for Autonomy. It describes pronounced difficulty with everyday demands, but its definition and assessment remain debated.

Read more: Understanding PDA

Why explore music?

For some children, music is an appealing way to participate, express ideas and connect. With the right support, it can offer a place to practise mistakes, choices and ways to get help. What works depends on the child and the setting.

Read more: Why Does Your School Need Music?

You can start the steps without reading the articles. Come back to the links whenever they are useful.

Step 1: What do you want the team to understand or change?

Your starting point might be explaining PDA and your child’s experience, asking for supports in general classrooms, including your child in music or adding music opportunities. Note strengths, what is difficult or missing, and what you hope could become possible.

Step 2: What could the team put in place?

Discuss the resources, adult support and access needed: a student profile, accessible materials, a discreet help signal, adapted music participation or a new inclusive music opportunity. Adults can help students learn to use the arrangements. Ask what is workable and how it can apply across settings.

Step 3: Start the conversation

Contact your child’s teacher or support coordinator. For program development, ask school leadership who should be involved. Share the student’s perspective, one example, your request and a relevant source. Ask the music educator to join where useful.

Download a short message to adapt

Step 4: Agree on support and review it

Write down what the team will provide or change, who will help, how students will learn to use it, access across settings, a backup and a review date. Include participation, useful choices, asking for help, musical learning and the support still needed.

Share only what helps explain the need.
