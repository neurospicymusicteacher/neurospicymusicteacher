My perspective and research approach

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent — not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm just a neurospicy person with intense hyperfocus, professional and lived experience, and access to the internet.

My articles explore research through an educational and lived-experience lens. They are intended to inform, encourage curiosity and offer practical ideas, not to provide clinical advice. I encourage you to explore the original research linked throughout.

What I bring to the conversation

I connect music education, special education, lived experience and persistent curiosity. I am exploring and interpreting research; I am not conducting the studies cited here.

Keep the types of information clear

Published findings are linked to their sources. Theory is named as theory. Failure First is my educational approach, informed by related evidence rather than independently validated. Practical suggestions and connections between fields are identified as interpretations or proposals.

Illustrative classroom situations are examples, not accounts of identifiable students. My lived experience is not everyone’s lived experience.

What verification means here

The bibliography records whether the check used a full text, abstract, original paper or official guidance. Checking a citation and its scope is not a new systematic review or an independent replication. Article drafts are dated so they can be revised as the evidence and our understanding change.

Mistakes are data. That applies to me, too.

I encourage readers to follow the original research, question my interpretations and share corrections. My goal is accessible information and more useful conversations about learning and belonging.

Every brain belongs in music.
