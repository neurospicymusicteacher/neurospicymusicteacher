A Proposed Six-Week Music Pilot

A planning framework to adapt together, not a validated treatment protocol.

Initial draft · 25 November 2026

Download PDF

Before the pilot

Choose one opportunity with the student and family. Name an adult coordinator, setting, schedule, sensory adjustments, a pause signal and a backup plan. For example, discuss 15–30 minutes two or three times a week if feasible; adjust to the person and setting.

Week 1: Listen and build trust

Identify interests, barriers and preferred ways to communicate. Record a brief starting picture with student input. Avoid requiring an audition or disclosure.

Week 2: Explore

Offer instruments, digital creation, chosen listening or observation. Teach how to use equipment safely. Check whether the invitation itself adds pressure.

Weeks 3–4: Develop

Support a chosen project and invite collaboration when welcomed. Adapt the amount, setting or feedback. Keep instruction available.

Week 5: Connect if useful

Explore links to peers, curriculum or an existing ensemble if the student wants them. More activity is not automatically a better outcome.

Week 6: Review and decide

Ask about musical growth, comfort, engagement, relationships and exhaustion after school. Attendance or quiet behaviour alone is insufficient. Continue, adapt or discontinue; identify who will follow through.

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent, not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm just a neurospicy person with intense hyperfocus, professional and lived experience, and access to the internet.
