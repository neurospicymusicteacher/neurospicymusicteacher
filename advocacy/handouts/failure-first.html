Failure First: A Staff Conversation

Sarah’s proposed approach: normalize mistakes and teach recovery.

Initial draft · 25 November 2026

Download PDF

Remove the moral judgement

“That entrance was late” describes an event. “You never try” judges a person. Keep the musical goal while making the next step teachable.

Use a recovery routine

Notice the attempt. Describe a barrier. Choose a strategy. Retry, adapt or pause. Reflect when the student is ready. Model your own ordinary learning mistakes without staging humiliating failures.

Offer concrete support

An illustrative response: “Would marking the rest or hearing the cue help?” Offer privacy and a manageable task. Repeating the same difficult task without support is not automatically productive learning.

Keep responsibility usable

Feedback, safety boundaries and repair still matter. Separate actions from identity, and support problem-solving at a workable time.

Evidence boundary

Error-climate, training and self-compassion research inform the approach. They do not independently validate Failure First in music or establish mental-health effects for students with PDA.

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent, not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm just a neurospicy person with intense hyperfocus, professional and lived experience, and access to the internet.

My articles explore research through an educational and lived-experience lens. They are intended to inform, encourage curiosity and offer practical ideas, not to provide clinical advice. I encourage you to explore the original research linked throughout.
