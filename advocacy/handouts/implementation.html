“Everyone Welcome. But Can Everyone Get In?”

Turn an invitation to music into something your child can actually use.

Download the one-page handout

1. Find the barrier at the door.

Is it the fee, instrument, noise, transport, audition or instructions? Does your child have to be calm and organised before they can enter the class that might help them feel connected? Ask them what makes joining difficult.

2. Ask what the school can change.

An instrument loan. A marked starting point. A predictable rehearsal plan. An agreed break and a way back in. If your child wants to play trumpet, explore support for trumpet before assigning them a completely different activity.

3. Make the arrangement specific.

Who helps? Where do they go? When is it available? What happens if the room is locked or the usual adult is away? Write it down. A support nobody can find is doing a fairly poor impression of support.

For a new music program...

Ask leadership about a qualified educator, teaching time, space, equipment and costs. A club can be a beginning. Check who can attend, especially if lunch is recovery time or transport rules out staying late.

Words you can borrow

What would need to be in place for my child, and other students with similar barriers, to take part?
