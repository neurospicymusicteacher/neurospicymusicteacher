Subject: Exploring music-based school supports

Hello,

I would like to explore the possibility of incorporating music-based supports into my child's educational plan.

For students with PDA profiles and other neurodivergent learners, traditional approaches to motivation, behaviour management and academic engagement can sometimes create additional barriers rather than remove them.

There is a growing body of research examining the potential benefits of music education and music-based interventions in areas such as emotional regulation, executive functioning, communication, social connection and engagement.

I have included some educational resources, research and practical programming suggestions for consideration.

I am not suggesting that music is a cure or that it should replace other necessary educational supports. Rather, I would like us to consider whether music could be used as an additional pathway to learning, regulation, connection and school belonging.

Some possibilities I would appreciate discussing include:
• Access to the music room as a supportive environment.
• Opportunities to participate in music classes or clubs with appropriate accommodations.
• Individual or small-group music-making opportunities.
• Music-based regulation breaks.
• Incorporating musical interests into curricular learning.
• A short pilot program to determine which supports are beneficial.

I would also like to discuss how access to these opportunities could be protected from becoming contingent upon behaviour, academic task completion or other unrelated expectations.

My hope is that we can identify approaches that build on my child's strengths and interests while supporting their well-being and capacity to participate in school.

I would appreciate the opportunity to meet with the school team, including the music educator where possible, to discuss what might be achievable within our existing resources.

Thank you for your consideration and willingness to explore additional ways to support my child.
