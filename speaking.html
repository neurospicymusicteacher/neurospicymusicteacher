Before the BELL

Practical, neuro-affirming teaching starts before the first day.

Before you step into your own classroom, make room for the human beings who will be in it—including you. This presentation gives future educators practical ways to build belonging, remove barriers, and create systems that support both the student and the teacher.

Drawing on 25 years of K–12 music teaching and Sarah’s lived experience with autism and ADHD, Before the Bell connects honest classroom stories with strategies you can take into your practicum and beyond. The examples come from the music room; the ideas reach across subjects.

Book Before the Bell

Virtual or in person · University classes and teacher education programs

What we explore together

  • Understand the brains in the room. Neurodiversity, spiky profiles, hyperfocus, and the difference between appearing to cope and feeling supported.
  • Support the educator, too. Tools for getting started, visible plans, routines, and sensory supports that make teaching more sustainable.
  • Look beneath behaviour. Treat mistakes as information, notice barriers, and respond with curiosity before trying to “fix” the student.
  • Build systems that do some of the work. Predictable starts, visual cues, organized materials, and clear procedures that reduce the load on everyone.
  • Help students take the next step. Meaningful choices, support for anxiety and autonomy, self-checks, and strategies that build independence.

Leave with a practical starting point for your own classroom and resources to explore as your teaching grows. Choose one small change, try it, and keep asking what would make learning more accessible.

Failure First: A Growth-Mindset Approach to Beginning Band

A practical beginner band session about helping students build confidence, resilience, and independence by normalizing mistakes, celebrating progress, and creating systems that support successful risk-taking from the very beginning.

Click for more ✨

Beginner band is messy, loud, exciting, and full of opportunities to make mistakes. Before students can create beautiful sounds, they need permission to squeak, miss notes, lose their place, try again, and discover that an imperfect attempt is evidence of learning—not proof that they “aren’t musical.”

In this session, Sarah shares practical ways to establish a growth-mindset culture before students ever put instruments in their hands. Participants will examine how language, routines, instrument selection, family communication, progress tracking, and classroom systems can reduce fear of failure and help young musicians become resilient problem-solvers.

The goal is not to lower expectations. It is to create the conditions students need to take healthy risks, respond to feedback, build independence, and keep going when learning feels difficult.

Best suited for

Beginner band teachers, elementary and junior high music teachers, new band teachers, music education conferences, professional learning days, and teachers rebuilding or rethinking their beginner band programs.

Key topics

Growth mindset, productive failure, beginner band setup, instrument selection, first sounds, resilience, student independence, parent communication, progress tracking, classroom culture, and support for students with additional needs.

From Squeaks to Symphonies

A realistic first-year beginner band sequence—from instrument selection and first sounds to sectionals and a functioning full ensemble.

Click for more ✨

Beginning band is not one moment when students receive instruments and start playing together. It is a carefully built sequence of small wins, clear routines, and increasingly complex ensemble experiences.

Sarah walks participants through the first-year arc: an instrument petting zoo, thoughtful instrument selection, Squeak & Squawk, split woodwind and brass instruction, sectionals, and the transition into full ensemble. Each stage is designed to protect early confidence while building the independence students need later.

Participants leave with a practical framework for pacing the year, teaching first sounds, managing mixed readiness, communicating with families, and creating early performances that feel successful without pretending the first year is polished or predictable.

Best suited for

Future music educators, beginning band teachers, elementary and junior high music teachers, new band teachers, conference sessions, and school divisions building or reviewing beginner band programs.

Key topics

Instrument petting zoo, instrument selection, first sounds, split woodwind and brass instruction, sectionals, full ensemble transition, pacing, early wins, family communication, and first-year performance planning.
