Want to Read the Research?

A bibliography and further reading for the articles and advocacy resources.

Welcome to the rabbit hole. These are the studies, reviews and resources I’ve been reading and connecting throughout this hub. Follow whatever catches your attention, look at the original work and make up your own mind. You don’t have to agree with every connection I’ve made.

Each entry has a short description to help you find a starting point. Some links open the full paper; others lead to an abstract or publisher’s page.

Longitudinal observational study

Dresel et al. (2025; first online 2024). Learning from errors in mathematics classrooms: Development over 2 years in dependence of perceived error climate. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 180–196.

Follows 1,641 students in 69 mathematics classrooms over two years, examining how classroom responses to errors relate to students’ responses to their own mistakes.

Meta-analysis

Keith & Frese (2008). Effectiveness of error management training: A meta-analysis. Journal of Applied Psychology, 93, 59–69.

Brings together 24 studies involving 2,183 participants. Explores learning through errors, including how people apply what they learn to new tasks.

Four experiments

Breines & Chen (2012). Self-compassion increases self-improvement motivation. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 38, 1133–1143.

Four experiments exploring whether a compassionate response to a personal shortcoming can support motivation to improve.

Adult singing study

Schladt et al. (2017). Choir versus Solo Singing: Effects on Mood, and Salivary Oxytocin and Cortisol Concentrations. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 11, 430.

Compares choir and solo singing in adults, including changes in mood, reported worry and hormone measures.

Three laboratory experiments

Schendel & Palmer (2007). Suppression effects on musical and verbal memory. Memory & Cognition, 35, 640–650.

Three laboratory experiments exploring how speaking and singing interact with memory for musical and verbal sequences.

Randomized experiment

Radstaak et al. (2014). Music and psychophysiological recovery from stress. Psychosomatic Medicine, 76, 529–537.

A study of 123 participants recovering from stress. Music improved mood; rumination did not significantly differ between recovery conditions.

Theoretical research review

Watkins (2008). Constructive and unconstructive repetitive thought. Psychological Bulletin, 134, 163–206.

A research review exploring when repetitive thinking is helpful and when it becomes unhelpful, including the role of thought content and context.

Laboratory experiments

Taruffi et al. (2017). Effects of Sad and Happy Music on Mind-Wandering and the Default Mode Network. Scientific Reports, 7, 14396.

Explores how sad and happy music affect mind-wandering and associated brain activity in laboratory settings.

Laboratory listening study

Koelsch et al. (2019). Heroic music stimulates empowering thoughts during mind-wandering. Scientific Reports, 9, 10317.

A listening study with 62 participants, exploring how heroic music influences the thoughts people report while their minds wander.

Systematic review

Kildahl et al. (2021). Pathological demand avoidance in children and adolescents: A systematic review. Autism, 25, 2162–2176.

Reviews 13 studies of demand avoidance in children and adolescents. Discusses how the proposed profile has been described and the research difficulties involved.

Systematic review of diagnostic evidence

Company & Rotella (2026). A Systematic Review of Pathological Demand Avoidance (PDA): A Veritable Diagnosis or a Case of Circular Logic? Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders.

Reviews 12 studies of PDA identification and diagnosis, including inconsistent definitions and methodological concerns.

Participatory qualitative research

Raymaker et al. (2020). “Having All of Your Internal Resources Exhausted Beyond Measure and Being Left with No Clean-Up Crew”: Defining Autistic Burnout. Autism in Adulthood, 2, 132–143.

Participatory research drawing on autistic adults’ accounts of burnout, including exhaustion, loss of skills and reduced tolerance to stimuli.

Historical theory

Maslow (1943). A theory of human motivation. Psychological Review, 50, 370–396.

The original paper on human motivation and needs, including the flexibility and exceptions within Maslow’s proposed ordering.

Meta-analysis

Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999). A meta-analytic review of experiments examining the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation. Psychological Bulletin, 125, 627–668.

A meta-analysis of 128 experiments examining rewards and intrinsic motivation. Findings vary with the type of reward and how motivation is measured.

Systematic review and meta-analysis

Jamey et al. (2024). Does music training improve inhibition control in children? A systematic review and meta-analysis. Cognition, 252, 105913.

A review of 22 controlled longitudinal studies, including eight randomized trials, examining music training and inhibitory control in children.

Review of a longitudinal program

Habibi et al. (2018). Music training and child development: A review of recent findings from a longitudinal study. Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences. DOI: 10.1111/nyas.13606.

Reviews findings from a longitudinal music-training program, including auditory development and related brain changes.

Systematic review and meta-analyses

de Witte et al. (2020; first online 2019). Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: A systematic review and two meta-analyses. Health Psychology Review, 14, 294–324.

Brings together 104 randomized trials of music interventions and psychological and physiological stress outcomes across varied settings.

Cochrane systematic review

Geretsegger et al. (2022). Music therapy for autistic people. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, CD004381.

A Cochrane review of 26 music-therapy studies with autistic participants. Reports probable benefits for overall improvement and quality of life, with uncertain findings for social interaction and communication.

Meta-analysis

Sala & Gobet (2020). Cognitive and academic benefits of music training with children: A multilevel meta-analysis. Memory & Cognition, 48, 1429–1441.

A meta-analysis examining claims that music training improves general cognition and academic achievement. Broad benefits were not found after accounting for study-design quality.

Teaching framework

CAST (2024). Universal Design for Learning Guidelines, version 3.0.

A practical framework for offering options in engagement, representation, and action or expression. Useful for thinking about access and participation.

Co-produced terminology guide

Autism Education Trust. Why Words Matter: Terminology Guide, co-produced with the Autistic Young Experts Panel, pp. 31 and 54.

A terminology guide co-produced with autistic young people. Includes different views about PDA language and why some prefer Persistent Drive for Autonomy.

Community and professional information

PDA Society. About demand avoidance.

Explores demands, including internal expectations and enjoyable activities, alongside accounts of what demand avoidance can feel like.

Where Else Could You Explore?

PDA Society has information, lived-experience perspectives and practical resources about PDA. The Canadian Association of Music Therapists is a starting point for learning about professional music therapy.
