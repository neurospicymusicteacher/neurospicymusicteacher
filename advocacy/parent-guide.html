“How Do I Get the School to Understand?”

PDA and neurodiversity support across classes, music inclusion and new school music programs.

You know your kid. Let’s turn that knowledge into a school conversation, with a few useful things to bring along.

Download the six-page parent guide

1. What is getting in the way?

Choose one starting point: understanding your child and PDA, support across classes, music inclusion or a new program. Bring your child’s perspective and one example the team can picture.

What is PDA? · Why start with music?

2. What would make this easier?

Ask for something adults can provide: spare materials without a public request, help learning a routine, an adapted music part, an instrument loan or teaching time for a new program. Name the goal and the support that could make it possible.

3. How do I start the conversation?

Contact your child’s teacher or support coordinator. For a new music program, ask school leadership who should be involved. Include the music educator where useful. Use the message below and choose one resource to attach.

Subject: Could we talk about [support / music access / a music program]? Hello [name], I’d like to talk about [what my child or the students need]. At the moment, [one example of what is getting in the way]. Could we explore [specific request]? My hope is that students can [participate, get help, learn music or another goal], with the resources and adult support they need. [My child says / we have noticed: ...] I’ve included [one article or handout] to help explain what I mean. I’d appreciate your ideas about what we could put in place. Could we agree on a first step, who will help and a time to check how it is working? Thank you, [Name]

Download the editable message

4. What did we agree to try?

Write down the change, who will provide it, when it starts, how your child will learn to use it and the backup plan. Agree on a review date and how your child will give feedback. Ask about participation, musical learning and exhaustion as well as completed work.
