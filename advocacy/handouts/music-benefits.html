“What Could Music Make Possible Here?”

A short research companion for families advocating for school music. Start with the musical learning.

Download the one-page handout

1. Name what students are missing.

A chance to play, compose, listen, perform or make music with other people. Those are worthwhile things to learn. Your child does not need a higher math mark to justify the instrument.

2. Choose research that fits the conversation.

Habibi and colleagues follow auditory learning and related brain changes. Jamey and colleagues examine inhibitory control: holding back an automatic response. Sala and Gobet examine broader academic claims. These are different questions, so choose the source that fits your request.

3. Connect it to an actual school opportunity.

Are you asking for inclusion in an existing class, an instrument loan, a beginner ensemble or a new program? Name the teaching and support needed. “Music is good for kids” is a starting point. A usable class needs time, resources and someone to teach it.

What about music therapy?

Music-therapy studies involve a different professional service. If you are asking for music education, focus on learning music, participation and access. You can discuss therapy separately when that is what your child needs.

Words you can borrow

We would like students to have access to [musical learning]. Could we discuss [teaching, resources and support] to make that possible?
