What Can Music Make Possible?

Research to read and share when you’re advocating for music education.

25 November 2026

Download PDF

What the research suggests

Controlled longitudinal studies support a possible benefit for inhibitory control. Longitudinal auditory findings and stress-intervention findings involve different activities and outcomes. None guarantees broad academic improvement.

What about grades and broader learning?

Music-related brain changes and school grades are different questions. Sala and Gobet examine broader academic and cognitive benefits and find that study design matters to the results. Musical learning has value even when grades elsewhere stay the same.

Keep services distinct

Music education teaches musical learning. Clinical music therapy requires qualified specialist practice. Cochrane findings about music therapy cannot automatically be applied to ordinary classroom music.

Ask about this student

Music may be enjoyable, expressive, overwhelming or some combination. Review the setting, volume, task and social demands with the student. Quiet is also a valid choice.

Protect the educational case

Students deserve opportunities to make and understand music whether or not other outcomes improve. Begin with musical learning, accessibility and the student’s interests.

A note about my perspective: I'm a music educator, special education specialist, neurodivergent human and parent, not a medical professional, psychologist or neuroscientist. I'm a neurospicy person with a lot of questions.

I bring professional and lived experience, intense hyperfocus and internet access. The sources are linked so you can keep reading, question my take and decide what’s useful for you.

Sources

  • Habibi et al. (2018). Music training and child development: A review of recent findings from a longitudinal study. Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences. DOI: 10.1111/nyas.13606.
  • de Witte et al. (2020; first online 2019). Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: A systematic review and two meta-analyses. Health Psychology Review, 14, 294–324.
  • Geretsegger et al. (2022). Music therapy for autistic people. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, CD004381.
  • Sala & Gobet (2020). Cognitive and academic benefits of music training with children: A multilevel meta-analysis. Memory & Cognition, 48, 1429–1441.
